Man-Bat Chronology As of June 2006
-----------------------------------

This pack includes the first issue of the new 2006 mini-series, both of the Man-Bat solo series, the Elseworlds stories and all key appearances to-date; it is based on the reference material at DCUGuide and RasAlGhul, but has been further expanded.  I also decided to include the full run (20 issues) of Batman Family for completists, even though Man-Bat didn´t appear with new stories until halfway through the run (11-20).

Overview from DCUGuide
http://www.dcuguide.com/who.php?name=manbat
An eminent biologist specialising in the study of bats, Kirk Langstrom longed to invent a serum which would enable the blind to mimic bats' natural sonar system. When he thought he had achieved success, Langstrom used himself as his first guinea pig, finding himself transformed into half-human/half-bat creature. Although this did give him the sonar ability he hoped for, it did also cause him to lose touch with his humanity. His wife Francine called Batman in for help, and an antidote was eventually discovered, Kirk then using the serum and antidote to do good as Man-Bat. He and Francine now have two children: Elizabeth and Aaron, the latter of whom has a man-bat form as his natural state.

Manbat/Kirk Langstrom Entry from RasAlGhul 
http://www.rasalghul.co.uk/characters_details.php?id=57
Dr. Kirk Langstrom, a scientist specializing in the study of bats, who developed an extract intended to give humans the bat's sonar sense. He tested the formula on himself, of course, and while it worked, it had a horrible side effect, transforming him gradually into a hideous human bat, complete with wings. He first clashed with Batman when he tried to steal the chemicals needed to reverse his transformation, but his control over the new animalistic instincts resulting from his bat-like state was tenuous, and Batman was forced to subdue him in order to administer the antidote.
Langstrom was cured, but still unbalanced; shortly afterwards, he transformed himself again and kidnapped his fiancée Francine, turning her into a human bat like himself with the intention of starting a new race of bat-like beings. Again, Batman was able to subdue the pair and return them to normal. (See 'TEC #400, #402, and #407; the first story is also retold in SECRET ORIGINS #39 and one of the 1995 "Year One" annuals recently reprinted in the FOUR OF THE KIND trade paperback).
Langstrom later refined his serum, enabling him to retain his human intelligence while in bat form, and allowing himself control over his transformations. He worked for a while as a detective and independent crime fighter as Man-Bat, while he and Francine married and had a daughter, Rebecca (circa BATMAN FAMILY #12-#20, 'TEC #485-490; he also had two issues of his own short-lived title).
Eventually, however, his psychological instability returned, and he was left unable to control his transformations, and maddened in his bat-form. In this condition, he clashed several times with Batman in the Batcave (which he found by following other bats through the network of caverns; he did not recall its location in human form, and he never knew Batman's secret identity), believing -- erroneously -- that his daughter was dead and that Batman was somehow responsible (BATMAN #348, #361/'TEC #527).
Recently, he regained control of his transformations, although his long-term stability is questionable.
The BATMAN/MAN-BAT mini-series, by Jamie Delano and John Bolton, is an Elseworlds showing Kirk and Francine, now living exclusively in bat form, with a teenaged son. Man-Bat, more unstable than ever (although at least coherent), was determined to replace the human race with a "superior" species of man-bat hybrids. There's nothing about this story that suggests it was originally intended to be an Elseworlds, which suggests that it was assigned that way because it effectively made Man-Bat into a Ra's al Ghul style megalomaniac villain, which apparently contradicted other plans for the character (I assume that it was already done, or mostly done, so they published it anyway and slapped the Elseworlds tag on it, as with GOLDEN AGE).
